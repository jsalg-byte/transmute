I would **not** use Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum → Diamond. That immediately drags the app back toward generic game/esports language.

For Transmute, I would make the rank ladder the **seven planetary metals plus the completed Stone**. Alchemy traditionally connected seven metals with seven planetary signs, which gives you a real historical system with recognizable glyphs and exactly enough material for an eight-rank ladder once the Philosopher’s Stone is added as the culmination. ([Encyclopedia.com][1])

I would probably use the simple English metal as the large name and the Latin or planetary association as the small editorial label.

## How the badges should look

Do not copy the faceted gems, wings, horns, skulls, shields, and neon colors from those references. That visual treatment would fight everything you have built.

Use one consistent **alchemical seal**:

* circular or slightly irregular engraved medallion
* central planetary glyph
* thin outer transmutation ring
* small Roman numeral
* material name below or around the perimeter
* more completed geometry at each rank
* restrained material texture rather than glossy gemstones

The silhouette should remain largely consistent. Progress comes from the seal becoming more complete:

1. **Lead:** broken outer circle, sparse linework
2. **Tin:** partial double ring
3. **Iron:** stronger angular construction
4. **Copper:** intersecting circle and line
5. **Mercury:** complete central geometry
6. **Silver:** complete pale seal with lunar ring
7. **Gold:** radiant solar marks
8. **The Stone:** completed ouroboros surrounding the squared circle

This will look much more like an evolving body of work than eight unrelated video-game badges.

## Best symbol choices

For the first seven, the traditional planetary glyphs are already excellent:

```text
Lead     ♄
Tin      ♃
Iron     ♂
Copper   ♀
Mercury  ☿
Silver   ☽
Gold     ☉
```

For **The Stone**, use one of these:

* squared circle
* circle containing a square and triangle
* completed ouroboros surrounding `☉`
* your existing ouroboros with a small central stone mark

The Unicode alchemical block also includes dedicated symbols for gold, silver, black sulfur, sublimation, distillation, dissolution, purification, putrefaction, crucibles, alembics and retorts. Use those references to find or redraw clean SVGs rather than relying on font rendering. ([Unicode][2])

Wikimedia Commons has a large alchemical-symbol collection suitable for locating individual SVG source material. Check each file’s licensing and normalize the stroke treatment yourself so the set looks designed together. ([Wikimedia Commons][3])

## Where the Magnum Opus colors belong

I would not rename the permanent ranks Nigredo, Albedo, Citrinitas and Rubedo. Those work better as **states of the larger journey**, because they describe transformative phases rather than achievement classes.

They could appear as division treatments:

* **RAW / Nigredo:** Lead and Tin
* **WORK / Albedo:** Iron and Copper
* **FORM / Citrinitas:** Mercury and Silver
* **PROOF / Rubedo:** Gold and The Stone

This is not a claim that the metals historically map neatly into those exact pairs; it is a branded interpretation for your product. Historically, black, white and red were major color stages, with alchemical traditions varying in how operations and stages were ordered. ([Encyclopedia.com][4])

My strongest recommendation is therefore:

**Lead → Tin → Iron → Copper → Mercury → Silver → Gold → The Stone**

with the badges designed as increasingly complete alchemical seals—not collectible gemstones.
